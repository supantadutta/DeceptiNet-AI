# Ethics, Legal Basis, and Containment

DeceptiNet-AI collects data about people attacking a system. That raises real
ethical and legal obligations. This document states them plainly. It is a living
document; sections marked *(Phase N)* describe controls that are planned but not
yet implemented — those gaps are also listed in [`LIMITATIONS.md`](LIMITATIONS.md).

## 1. Deployment authorization (read this first)

**Do not deploy DeceptiNet-AI on any network you are not explicitly authorized
to instrument.**

- Deploying on a **corporate, university, or other production network** without
  **written authorization** may violate acceptable-use policies, employment
  agreements, and computer-misuse / wiretapping laws. Get written sign-off.
- A honeypot intercepts and records third-party communications. In some
  jurisdictions this implicates wiretap/interception statutes. Understand your
  local law before collecting.
- For a thesis: obtain your institution's **ethics-board / IRB** determination
  before public deployment. Honeypot attacker data is typically about
  machines/automated actors, but it can incidentally contain personal data.

## 2. Containment (spec §2.3) — what enforces "this cannot harm third parties"

1. **No real command execution, ever.** There is no real shell, filesystem, or
   service. Every response is synthesised from an in-memory virtual filesystem.
   This is proven by `tests/test_no_real_execution.py` (behavioural + static
   source scan). This is the single most important control: it is what makes
   this a honeypot rather than a compromised box that could attack others.
2. **Default-deny egress.** In Docker, the honeypot attaches **only** to an
   `internal: true` network, which has no route to the internet. It can reach
   the local datastore and nothing else. `containment.egress` must be `deny` or
   `allow_llm_only`; the value `allow` is rejected by config validation.
   - The runtime `probe_egress()` tripwire (surfaced on `/health` in later
     phases) is *evidence, not proof* — the network-level default-deny is the
     real control.
   - `allow_llm_only` (reaching an LLM endpoint for Phase 2) is **not yet
     wired**; it will require an explicit egress proxy/allowlist, not blanket
     outbound. *(Phase 2)*
3. **Kill switch.** A single file-based switch stops all exposed listeners
   (`make kill`). It works even if the process is otherwise busy.
4. **`wget`/`curl` never fetch.** Malware-staging commands are captured for
   intelligence but produce an inert, non-networked response.

## 3. Data handling

Captured data **will** contain attacker-supplied credentials and may contain
credentials belonging to **third parties**, PII, or references to victim
infrastructure.

- **Storage location:** the datastore (SQLite/Postgres), never the operational
  logs. Operational logs (JSON to stdout) record metadata (source IP, event
  types) but not, e.g., full payload bodies beyond what is needed.
- **Encryption / hashing at rest:** ⚠️ **NOT YET IMPLEMENTED.** Credentials and
  command content are currently stored **in plaintext**. Hashing/encryption at
  rest is a required pre-public-deployment hardening item. *(Phase 7)* Until
  then, treat the datastore as sensitive: restrict access, encrypt the volume at
  the OS level, and do not commit it (it is `.gitignore`d).
- **Retention & scrubbing:** define a retention window appropriate to your
  ethics approval and delete beyond it. A documented, automated
  retention/scrubbing job is *(Phase 7)*. Until then, retention is manual.
- **Minimization:** only SSH interaction data is collected in Phase 1.

## 4. Responsible disclosure of captured zero-days

If captured attacker activity reveals a previously-unknown vulnerability or a
live victim being attacked:

- Do not weaponize or republish exploit details.
- Report through coordinated disclosure to the affected vendor / CERT.
- If a specific third party appears to be an active victim, prioritize notifying
  them or the relevant CERT.

## 5. What this project deliberately does **not** do

- It does not attack back, scan, or probe any external host.
- It does not execute, store-and-forward, or relay attacker payloads.
- It does not retain data it has no use for beyond the stated research purpose.

## 6. Honesty mandate

Per the build spec: this project must not fabricate attacker data, benchmark
numbers, or results. Test/demo sessions are generated by our **own** client
connecting to the honeypot and are labelled as such — they are real captures of
a real (benign, self-originated) connection, never invented rows.
